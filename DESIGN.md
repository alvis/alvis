## Overview

Alvis is an editorial personal site with a watercolor field-note sensibility. It should feel like a thinking desk: warm paper, ink lines, personal marks, annotated diagrams, and enough structure to make the work feel rigorous.

The taste-card reference is saved at [public/images/taste-card/webpage-taste-card.png](/Users/alvis/Repositories/alvis/public/images/taste-card/webpage-taste-card.png). Use it as the primary visual reference for page rhythm, responsive blog layouts, portrait placement, and the refined nav/favicon mark.

The site voice is personal, concise, and reflective. It should not feel like a SaaS landing page or a portfolio template. The hero line should stay short and human; the writing section should feel like a small publication rather than a list of generic cards.

## Colors

The palette is warm paper plus deep navy ink, supported by restrained watercolor accents.

- **Primary (#162747):** main text, large headlines, icons, arrows, and diagram strokes.
- **Secondary (#506176):** supporting text, annotations, captions, and quiet metadata.
- **Tertiary / Oxide (#A64B2A):** active states, underlines, dates, and small editorial emphasis.
- **Neutral (#F7F1E6):** page foundation, warmer than white and never flat.
- **Surface (#FFF9EE):** raised paper cards, mockup surfaces, and content panels.
- **Border (#D8C9B4):** thin editorial dividers and soft containment lines.
- **Accent Blue, Olive, Amber:** watercolor washes, diagram fills, tag variation, and blog imagery.

Avoid large monochrome gradients, glossy surfaces, neon accents, pure black text, and stark white backgrounds. Color should look applied by ink and pigment, not generated by a dashboard theme.

## Typography

Use expressive serif typography for the site’s voice and compact sans-serif labels for structure. Headlines should be large, calm, and literary, with normal letter spacing. Body copy should read like essay text: generous line height, narrow enough measures, and no marketing compression.

Use `label-caps` for dates, categories, nav microcopy, and diagram labels. Keep label copy sparse. Do not use oversized type inside compact cards or controls.

## Layout

The default composition is editorial and responsive:

- Desktop uses a true webpage composition with a top hero, a visible writing section below, wide gutters, thin rules, and image/text blog rows.
- Tablet keeps the same order but tightens image scale, excerpt length, and navigation spacing.
- Mobile becomes a single-column reading flow with a compact header, the portrait below the hero copy, and stacked writing items.

The hero must sit above the writing section. The portrait should use the existing profile image at [public/images/profile/me-philosophical.png](/Users/alvis/Repositories/alvis/public/images/profile/me-philosophical.png), treated like a watercolor cutout on paper.

Use stable spacing and fixed-format image regions so cards and rows do not jump when text length changes. Dividers should do most of the structural work; avoid heavy boxed sections.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth should be paper-like: subtle shadows, visible paper edges, and watercolor texture. Use soft elevation only for full mockup cards, modals, or paper fragments. Avoid glossy shadows, glass effects, bokeh, decorative blobs, and nested card stacks.

Watercolor washes may sit behind diagrams and images, but they should not reduce legibility or become a generic background decoration.

## Shapes

Corners are compact: 4px for small controls, 8px for cards and paper panels, and 12px only for large mockup containers. Thin circular or hand-drawn forms are appropriate inside diagrams and the favicon mark.

The nav logo/favicon should refine the existing glasses-and-squiggle character: round glasses, ink outline, small hand-drawn facial mark, and subtle purple-blue watercolor fill. It should remain recognizable at favicon size and feel personal, curious, and handmade.

## Components

Header: show the refined emblem with the `ALVIS` wordmark on desktop. On tablet and mobile, keep the emblem compact and recognizable. Use social icons on the top right: GitHub, LinkedIn, X/Twitter, and email. Do not use a light/dark toggle in this direction.

Hero: use the short line `I build systems that help ideas become real.` with supporting copy `Product, AI, and writing shaped by curiosity, context, and craft.` Keep calls to action quiet, text-based, and underlined rather than button-heavy.

Writing list: posts should use real titles, dates, categories, and matching watercolor hero imagery. Blog images should behave like field notes or small diagrams:

- `The Context Layer Is the Product` uses a context systems diagram.
- `Why Agent Demos Break in Production` uses an agent stack with memory, tools, guardrails, and real-world feedback.
- `Designing Tools That Disappear Into the Work` uses a workflow/interface sketch fading into notes.

## Do's and Don'ts

Do:

- Use the saved taste card as the visual north star for layout, image treatment, and responsive rhythm.
- Keep copy personal, specific, and calm.
- Use thin rules, editorial spacing, and watercolor diagrams to create structure.
- Preserve the existing profile portrait and refined glasses emblem as identity anchors.

Don't:

- Replace the portrait with a generic avatar or photoreal person.
- Use stock thumbnails for blog posts.
- Reintroduce a light/dark mode switch in the header mockup.
- Turn the site into a generic app landing page with large buttons, glossy cards, or feature grids.
- Let text overlap, scale with viewport width, or become too small to read on mobile.
